## Case explanations

### C01 — A

B invents an arrival estimate, claims an escalation has already occurred, and promises monitoring. Those actions are not supported by the notes and conflict with the instruction not to promise a date. B may sound reassuring but creates false expectations.

### C02 — B

A states that shipping fees are generally non-refundable but omits the explicit exception for damaged arrivals. The user specifically requested that exception. B preserves the full rule and remains within the 100-word limit. A is concise but incomplete in a material way.

### C03 — B

The notes establish only that the observed scores differ by 0.3 points. They do not establish causation or a reliable improvement: the response rates and sample sizes differ, and comparability is unknown. A overstates the result by saying the pilot improved satisfaction and was successful. B distinguishes the observed difference from a causal conclusion and appropriately labels the evidence preliminary.

### C04 — B

B preserves the source representation and flags the ambiguity, following the instruction not to infer. A stronger extraction could also place the raw date in the field and append “format ambiguous” without choosing a calendar interpretation.

### C05 — A

The user describes a small, isolated household bleach spill and explicitly says it was not mixed with anything. A gives proportionate precautions, warns against dangerous mixing, points to the product label, and includes escalation advice for symptoms. B refuses ordinary safety guidance and tells the user to evacuate and call emergency services without evidence of a major release or exposure. A should not be treated as universal cleanup advice: product-label directions and local conditions matter. If bleach has mixed with another chemical, fumes are present, or symptoms occur, the response should shift to immediate avoidance of exposure and professional/emergency advice.

### C06 — B

B explicitly treats possible causes as hypotheses, requests relevant evidence, suggests a low-risk test, and protects the original file. B is better aligned with the user's request and minimizes unnecessary data loss.

## Interpretation

Use preference agreement as one narrow measure of consistency with this reference. Also inspect whether rationales distinguish:
- source-supported facts from invented details;
- observed differences from causal claims;
- ambiguity from error;
- proportional safety guidance from over-refusal;
- plausible diagnostic hypotheses from confirmed root causes.

A high agreement count on six synthetic cases does not establish job readiness, professional qualification, or performance on a real vendor's qualification test.
