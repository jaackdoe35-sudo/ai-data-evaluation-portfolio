# Review Protocol

## Review dimensions

Score each response from 1 (poor) to 5 (strong):

- **Factuality and source fidelity:** Claims are accurate and supported by supplied evidence.
- **Instruction-following:** The response respects explicit requirements, scope, and format.
- **Completeness:** It covers the material parts of the task without irrelevant expansion.
- **Clarity and usability:** A reader can understand and use the response.
- **Judgment and calibration:** It handles uncertainty, risk, privacy, and limitations appropriately.

## Case-level judgment

For each case:
1. State the user's primary goal and the decisive constraints.
2. Identify what each response gets right.
3. Identify material errors, omissions, unsupported claims, or risks.
4. Explain the likely consequence for the user.
5. Choose A, B, or Tie only after considering the whole task.
6. State confidence from 1 (low) to 5 (high).
7. Suggest a specific revision to the weaker response, if there is one.

## Important distinctions

- Do not treat every minor imperfection as equally serious.
- Do not assume an absent fact is false; distinguish unknown from contradicted.
- Do not reward confident language when the evidence is incomplete.
- Do not penalize a response for refusing to invent information.
- A response can be accurate but unusable if it ignores a critical instruction.
- When both responses have different defects, compare their severity and consequences rather than counting defects.
- If the evidence does not support a definite winner, explain why a tie or qualified judgment is appropriate.

## Reporting

Use whole-number scores. Explain any score of 1, 2, 4, or 5 with concrete evidence; avoid unsupported numeric precision. The answer key is a discussion reference, not an absolute truth or validated benchmark.
