## Case-by-case rationale

### C01 — A

Correct calculation:
- Notebooks: 8 × ₦1,250 = ₦10,000.
- Pens: 5 × ₦400 = ₦2,000.
- Total: ₦12,000.

A reports both subtotals correctly (₦10,000 and ₦2,000) but adds them incorrectly as ₦12,500. B reports the pen subtotal incorrectly as ₦2,500 and also gives the incorrect total ₦12,500. A is materially better because its component calculations are accurate and only the final addition is wrong; B contains an additional arithmetic error. The reference preference is A. A strong evaluator should identify the distinct error locations and their consequences rather than merely count errors.

### C02 — A

The user explicitly requested exactly three bullet points. A complies and accurately represents the four source facts by combining implementation and testing in one bullet. B is factually accurate but uses four bullets, violating a clear, measurable instruction. A is preferred on instruction-following; the case illustrates that truth alone is not sufficient for task success.

### C03 — A

The excerpt contains no 48-hour deadline. B adds a specific procedural requirement that is unsupported by the source. The invented detail could cause employees to submit requests earlier than required or misunderstand the policy. A is faithful to the supplied text and is preferred.

### C04 — A

15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30. The task is deterministic and the information is sufficient. B's “probably” and claim that a calculator is needed signal uncertainty that is not warranted. Appropriate calibration means expressing uncertainty when evidence is incomplete—not hedging every answer.

### C05 — A

The percentage decrease is calculated relative to the starting value:
(100 − 80) ÷ 100 × 100 = 20%.

A gives a correct and consistent calculation. B first divides by 80, which produces 25%, then states 20% using a different rationale. Its final answer happens to be correct, but the displayed calculation is invalid and the explanation is internally inconsistent. A is preferred. An evaluator should not overlook defective reasoning merely because the final number is right.

## How to interpret your result

Compare your preferences and rationales with the reference judgments. Count preference agreement out of five, but do not treat that figure as a professional qualification score. Review dimension scores separately: the exercise is intended to reveal reasoning patterns, not establish a statistically reliable proficiency level from five cases.

For a useful self-review, ask:
1. Did I identify that A is better in C01 because its subtotals are correct, even though its final addition is wrong?
2. Did I enforce the exact constraint in C02?
3. Did I detect the unsupported detail in C03?
4. Did I penalize unjustified hedging in C04?
5. Did I inspect the calculation rather than only the final answer in C05?
