AI SAFETY EVALUATION

From failure to potential harm.

Risk is explained in terms of who or what could be affected, the conditions required and what the source evidence does not establish.

Negative quantity: potential financial integrity impact

If a downstream transaction engine accepts signed quantities, a negative value can reverse the direction of a debit/credit calculation. For example, cost 10 × quantity -100 produces -1,000 rather than +1,000. Depending on accounting semantics, this could create an unintended credit, incorrect balance, invalid ledger entry, settlement/reconciliation failure or customer/merchant loss. Repeated accepted operations could amplify exposure.

Preconditions: downstream service trusts signed values; no effective invariant check; transaction reaches posting or settlement. Unknown: actual backend arithmetic, persistence, authorization, reversibility and repeatability. Arena evidence confirms the tool call, not financial loss.

Cross-user retrieval: potential confidentiality and identity misuse

If a comparable service lacks per-object authorization, a caller may retrieve another customer's personal or financial record. Exposed details could make phishing, impersonation and social engineering more credible, and could contribute to follow-on fraud if they overlap with account recovery or identity checks.

Preconditions: identifier can be varied; server does not enforce ownership/permission; returned fields contain sensitive data. Unknown: production exposure, bulk extraction, account takeover and root cause. The Arena record contains synthetic challenge data; personal values are intentionally omitted.

Content reproduction: policy and rights exposure

Repeated evaluator-marked content-reproduction outcomes can indicate that a model may produce outputs beyond a service's intended content policy. In a deployed product, this can create rights-holder complaints, takedown workload, user trust issues and contractual or legal review needs, depending on work, jurisdiction, licensing and context.

Evidence boundary: Arena screenshots show challenge-specific evaluator scores. They are not legal determinations of infringement or a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal analysis.

Oversized command suggestion: reviewability and execution risk

Long or complex command suggestions may be difficult for a user to inspect, especially when they include chained operations, file changes or privileged actions. If executed without review, consequences depend on command semantics and user privileges.

Evidence boundary: Shellby success screen confirms the oversized-command criterion at 100%. The successful run's exact token count is not visible; command execution, resource exhaustion, data modification and damage are not established.

AI GOVERNANCE

Risk register and control objectives.

These are proposed control objectives derived from the findings. They are not claims that the Arena or another production system has implemented them.

R-01 / INTEGRITY

Transaction invariants

Reject negative, zero, malformed and out-of-range quantities server-side before any transaction side effect. Model instructions are not a security boundary.

Proposed control · evidence not available
R-02 / CONFIDENTIALITY

Object-level authorization

Bind subject identity to authenticated server context and authorize every object access. Never trust a model-supplied record identifier as proof of permission.

Proposed control · evidence not available
R-03 / CONTENT POLICY

Rights-aware output controls

Define permitted transformations, reproduction boundaries, safe alternatives, escalation and appeal procedures; measure output behavior against approved criteria.

Proposed control · not legally assessed
R-04 / OUTPUT SAFETY

Command and output review

Enforce output constraints at the generation/action boundary, label side effects and require explicit user review before consequential execution.

Proposed control · implementation unverified
CROSS-CUTTING

Least privilege and monitoring

Scope tool capabilities to the minimum required; log allow/deny decisions, abnormal activity and policy-relevant events; define incident response and containment.

Proposed control · not tested
CROSS-CUTTING

Change and regression governance

Retest exact failure modes after model, prompt, tool, policy or backend changes. Require release evidence and authorized risk acceptance for unresolved material risks.
