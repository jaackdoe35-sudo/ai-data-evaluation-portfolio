02 / Interactive demonstration

Make an evaluation decision.

A short synthetic example showing how a reviewer can compare an answer with the user's actual request. Select the response that better satisfies the instruction.

Your task

Read for correctness, not polish.

A user asks for a refund of a $24 item. The policy excerpt says refunds are available within 30 days when the item is unused. The purchase was 12 days ago and the item is unused.

  • Check whether the answer uses the supplied facts.
  • Check whether it follows the stated policy.
  • Do not reward confident wording if the conclusion is wrong.

Synthetic training example. This is not a real customer interaction.

User request / policy context
“Can I get a refund for my $24 item? I bought it 12 days ago and it is unused.”

Policy: Refunds are available within 30 days for unused items.
Choose the better response
RESPONSE A

“Yes. Based on the policy you provided, the item meets the 30-day and unused-item conditions. The $24 purchase is within the stated eligibility window.”

RESPONSE B

“No. Refunds are only available during the first 7 days, even if the item has not been used.”

Select a judgment to reveal the evidence-based review.
03 / Working method

Make the decision traceable.

The goal is not to make every judgment sound certain. It is to make each decision understandable, reviewable and appropriately qualified.

01

Parse the task

Extract instructions, schema, constraints and the actual unit of work.

02

Apply the rubric

Use explicit label definitions and task-specific evaluation criteria.

03

Record evidence

Write concise rationales tied to observable source information.

04

Review & flag

Validate structure, document ambiguity and escalate unresolved cases.
